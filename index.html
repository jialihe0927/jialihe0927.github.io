Research

Pseudo-marginal Sampling for Selective Inference

Research with Sifan Liu, Duke University · April 2026–Present

  • Implemented independent and correlated pseudo-marginal MCMC samplers that use simulated selection outcomes for selective inference with analytically intractable selection probabilities.
  • Designed and ran all simulations to assess sampling efficiency, Type I error, and power in a Gaussian file-drawer benchmark, cross-validated lasso, and randomized changepoint detection.
  • Measured several-hundred-fold gains in sampling efficiency over rejection sampling in some settings.
  • Drafted the manuscript and checked proofs of cost bounds and sample complexity results.

Prediction-Oriented Multivariate Shuffled Linear Regression

Undergraduate thesis advised by Qing Yang, USTC · September 2026–Present

  • Proposed studying how residual predictor--response mismatches affect out-of-sample prediction risk.
  • Derived risk decompositions and proved sufficient conditions for consistency and first-order oracle equivalence.
  • Designed controlled experiments and wrote simulation code to test the theoretical predictions.
  • Extending the analysis to high-dimensional settings.

Return Subsidies and Quality Signaling in Livestream Commerce

Project Lead, National Undergraduate Innovation Training Program · October 2025–May 2026

Advisor: Prof. Jie Wu, USTC; Postdoctoral Collaborator: Xiongfei Guo, USTC

  • Formulated a Bayesian signaling game linking return subsidies to consumers' beliefs about product quality.
  • Derived purchase and return thresholds and proved the existence of a least-cost separating equilibrium.
  • Documented the findings in a complete research manuscript, which was incorporated into the team's final report.
  • Led a three-person team, coordinated meetings and progress, and presented at the final defense; the project received RMB 20,000 in funding and an Excellent rating.
